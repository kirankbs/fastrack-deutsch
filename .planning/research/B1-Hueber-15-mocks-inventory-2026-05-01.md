## Cross-mock patterns

### Distractor design

- **Numerical near-miss.** Wrong answers swap an exact figure (3000 → 30000 visitors; 18 → 17; 5 days → 6 days). Lifted directly from the text but in a subtly altered context. Always test which sentence the figure originally modified.
- **Subject swap on similar surface.** Among "die Eltern / die Lehrer / die Mitschüler" wrong options ascribe the action to the wrong agent who is mentioned elsewhere in the same paragraph.
- **Generalisation traps.** Text says "manche", distractor says "alle"; text says "selten", distractor says "nie". Watch all-quantifiers vs hedged ones.
- **Causal reversal.** Wrong answer reverses cause/effect ("X weil Y" inverted to "Y weil X") — frequent in T2 articles where two related facts coexist.
- **Time-frame trap.** Text says "wird … sein" (planned), distractor says "ist bereits" (already done). Or "vor drei Jahren" mixed with "seit drei Jahren".
- **False inference from titles.** Sentence sounds like a plausible takeaway but isn't supported. The Hueber answer key is conservative — only what is explicitly said counts.
- **Lexical false friends.** "kostenlos" vs "billig", "verboten" vs "nicht erlaubt", "berufstätig" vs "Berufsausbildung" — surface synonyms with subtly different scopes.
- **Verb voice / mood swap in cloze.** A common Sprachbausteine 1 distractor: "wurde" vs "wäre" vs "würde"; "sei" vs "war"; "müsste" vs "müsste haben". Tests Konjunktiv I/II discrimination.
- **Preposition micro-traps.** an/auf/in/zu/nach with motion vs location; bei/mit/seit (temporal seit + Dat.); wegen + Gen. vs wegen + Dat.

### Hueber stylistic markers

- **Voice is journalistic-warm**, not academic. Articles open with a concrete scene — a child's tightrope walk, a dog at a cheese counter, a senior in a cinema — then zoom out to the general phenomenon. Three-act mini-structure: anecdote → expert quote → broader trend.
- **Direct quotes from named professionals.** Almost every T2 article carries 2–3 quotes attributed to a real-sounding named person (Dr. Rosina, Stephan Schiller, Professor Hans Günther Bastian). Builds authority and breaks up exposition.
- **Sentence rhythm is varied** — short punchy openers ("Spaß hat es gemacht.") followed by longer subordinated sentences. AI tends to write uniformly medium-length; Hueber alternates.
- **Modal-particle texture.** Generous use of *eigentlich, doch, ja, schon, halt, nun, also, übrigens* in personal letters — these are scarce in AI-default German.
- **Concrete brand names and currencies.** Real magazines (Bravo, Brigitte, Stadtrevue), real organisations (Greenpeace, BUND, ARCHE NOAH, SOS-Kinderdorf), Euro/DM/franc figures. Lends authenticity but also dates the material to ~1998–2008.
- **Idioms used sparingly but distinctively.** "aus der Patsche helfen", "auf die Sprünge helfen", "es regnet nicht mehr in Strömen", "Ende gut, alles gut", "auf eigene Faust", "kreuz und quer". Roughly 1–2 idioms per mock — never overcooked.
- **Light humour and rhetorical questions.** "Wer putzt die Tafel — der Lehrer oder die Schüler?" "Beispiel gefällig?" Headlines often pun (Tauschen statt Kaufen; Verbrecherjagd mit Bleistift und Maus).
- **Era-specific lexis.** Frequent: Stereoanlage, Walkman, Tonband, Disketten, "ankreuzen", "Anzeige aufgeben", Telefonzelle, Au-pair, Ostsee/Nordsee, Mark-era prices. C1 / modern terms like *Influencer, Streaming, Klimakrise* are absent.

### Difficulty calibration across the 15 mocks

Hueber does not produce a strict linear ramp. Topical complexity rises gently:

- **Mocks 1–4** lean teen-life, concrete actions, frequent vocabulary. T2 articles average ~450–500 words on tangible topics (circus, clown therapy, jugendreisen, service dogs).
- **Mocks 5–9** introduce more abstract framings — memory and learning theory (5), women's automotive self-help (8), exchange economies (9). T2 articles average ~480–530 words. Idioms appear more often. Sprachbausteine word-bank requires more conjunction discrimination (sodass, jedoch, falls).
- **Mocks 10–15** show modest abstract-vocabulary bumps:
  - Sociological/critical pieces (lying psychology Mock 11, dwarfism advocacy Mock 12, child-labour activism Mock 15) bring nominalisations (Bagatellisierung, Selbstständigkeit, Geltungssucht, Chancengleichheit, Ausbeutung).
  - Konjunktiv I and Plusquamperfekt-Passiv usage rises in T1 cloze.
  - Distractor wording becomes more paraphrased — fewer literal-word matches with the source text.
  - Schreiben prompts shift from pure "tell me about your daily life" to more reactive scenarios (apartment search, complaint about defective product).

The progression is real but soft — a strong B1+ candidate would not fail Mock 15 while passing Mock 1.

### Schreiben prompts — letter-type distribution

Of 15 prompts:

- **Private letter / email to friend or penpal (informal-affective):** Mocks 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15 — **10 of 15.** Topics: daily routine, holiday camp, new partner, hobbies, cinema, holiday photos, problem-neighbours, language course in Munich, moving to countryside, plans to live abroad.
- **Reply to a relative (parents/family) on travel:** Mock 2 (parents in Tokyo).
- **Reply to a host family / acquaintance about a forgotten item:** Mock 5 (sunglasses).
- **Reply to a holiday-house owner / advertiser (semi-formal inquiry):** Mocks 3, 7, 11.
- **Semi-formal complaint:** Mock 14 (defective computer game return — closest to "Beschwerdebrief").
- **Reply to invitation:** Mocks 3 (mountain holiday) and 4 (new partner reaction) shade towards invitation-response.

Pure formal complaints (e.g. defective product) are **rare — only Mock 14**. Most prompts demand the "personal letter with 4 bullets, opening, closing, date" template. Hueber heavily privileges the friend-to-friend register over Beschwerde.

**Take-aways for our equivalent 15 mocks:** lead with party + trip scenarios, mix in 1–2 "help/care" tasks, 1 "create something new" task, avoid niche themes. Drop ≥1 "school exhibition / project" since it gives strong vocab leverage on Veranstaltung organisation lexis.
